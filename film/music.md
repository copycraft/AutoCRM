# AutoCRM film: music

The music carries the film where the narration pauses (about a third of the 6:00), and stays
out of its way the rest of the time. It is dipped 8 dB under every narration line
([motion.md](motion.md), Sound).

## What any track must have

- **No vocals**, and a sparse middle register: the narration lives at 1–4 kHz.
- **A steady pulse at 90–110 BPM**, so cuts and camera moves can land on the beat.
- **Length:** 6:00 is long for one track. It needs stems or a loop or extended version, so it
  can be cut to length on bar lines without a hole.
- **A real ending** (a last chord or hit, not a fade), to land on the end card at 6:00.
- **A licence that covers a client video**, and YouTube if the film ever goes there.

## How it follows the film

| Film | Scenes | Music |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Opening, title | Sparse intro that swells into the title; at full level (−20 LUFS) |
| 0:20–1:40 | Enquiry, quote, order | Light bed: the motif, no drums |
| 1:40–3:10 | Phone, átvétel, photos | The rhythm comes in: the shop floor, things happening |
| 3:10–4:05 | Production, yard, TV | Keeps that energy, one layer more |
| 4:05–5:35 | Billing, mail, HR, overview | Pulls back to the light bed |
| 5:35–6:00 | Handover, end card | Lift on the signature, resolve on the end card |

## Four directions

1. **Hideg precizitás** (my pick): a felt-piano motif over a soft analogue pulse, light
   shaker and rim, ~96–100 BPM, major with a slightly wistful edge. Calm and exact, like the
   work itself. Style: modern-classical-with-a-beat, Ólafur Arnalds territory, not stock
   "corporate piano".
2. **Műhely:** organic and hand-made: muted electric guitar picking, warm bass, brushed
   drums, a few hand claps, ~100–105 BPM. Feels like craft and workshops more than software.
3. **Tiszta tech:** plucked synths, round sub bass, gently pumping pads, ~110 BPM, Tycho or
   Bonobo-like chill electronic. Modern and clean, but the closest to generic SaaS videos.
4. **Szegedi szín** (wildcard): direction 1 or 3 with a short **cimbalom** motif only in the
   opening and on the end card. It gives a local signature without folk kitsch, if kept to
   a few notes. Stock tracks with cimbalom are rare, so this likely means a stem track plus
   a cimbalom sample, or a composer.

## Where to look

- **Artlist or Epidemic Sound:** subscriptions that clearly cover client work, with stems.
  Search: "minimal piano pulse", "modern documentary", "inspiring minimal technology",
  "acoustic groove", "chill electronic plucks".
- **Pixabay Music:** free for commercial use without credit; same search words. Check each
  track for YouTube Content ID before using it there.

## Sound effects (very quiet, −30 dB)

A soft click for clicks and taps, a short tick for green ticks, a low whoosh for scene
changes, a notification chime (scene 1), a camera shutter on the phone's photos (scenes 5–6),
and a faint pen-on-glass for the signatures. A CC0 pack or the same subscription covers them.
